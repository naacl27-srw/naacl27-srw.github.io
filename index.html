Welcome

Forum for student research, mentorship, and community at NAACL 2027.

The NAACL 2027 Student Research Workshop (SRW) will be held in conjunction with NAACL 2027 in San Francisco, California, from June 1-5, 2027. The SRW brings together students working across computational linguistics, natural language processing, and machine learning.

The workshop provides a supportive venue for students to present research, receive feedback from the international NLP community, meet mentors and peers, and learn how to develop, evaluate, and communicate high-quality research. We welcome students at multiple stages, including high school, undergraduate, MSc/MA, and PhD students.

Call for Papers

Student research papers and thesis proposals for NAACL 2027 SRW.

About the Student Research Workshop

The NAACL 2027 Student Research Workshop is a forum for student-led work in computational linguistics, natural language processing, and closely related areas of machine learning. Participants can present completed work, work in progress, or thesis proposals and receive feedback from reviewers, mentors, and the broader NAACL community.

Submission Categories

Thesis Proposals

Appropriate for PhD students who have identified a thesis topic and would like feedback on the proposed direction, research plan, and broader framing.

Research Papers

Appropriate for completed work or work in progress with preliminary results. The first author must be a current student or must have completed the work while a student.

Archival and Non-Archival Tracks

Submissions may be marked as archival or non-archival according to the authors' preference. Accepted archival papers are expected to appear in the official workshop proceedings. Accepted non-archival papers may be presented at the workshop without proceedings publication and may be submitted to future venues, subject to those venues' policies.

Important: a submission marked as non-archival may not be convertible to archival after acceptance unless the final workshop policy explicitly allows it.

Submission Route

NAACL 2027 SRW is currently planned as a direct-submission-only workshop. Papers should be submitted through the SRW submission site when it opens. An ARR commitment track is not planned unless a later official announcement states otherwise.

Topics of Interest

Topics are expected to follow the broad scope of NAACL and ACL venues, including but not limited to:

  • Computational social science and social media
  • Dialogue and interactive systems
  • Discourse, pragmatics, syntax, semantics, morphology, and linguistic theory
  • Ethics, responsible NLP, safety, fairness, privacy, and policy
  • Information extraction, information retrieval, and text mining
  • Interpretability, analysis, and evaluation of NLP models
  • Language grounding, multimodality, speech, vision, and robotics
  • Large language models, agents, tool use, reasoning, and adaptation
  • Machine learning for NLP, multilinguality, and machine translation
  • NLP applications, question answering, summarization, resources, and benchmarks
  • Sentiment analysis, stylistic analysis, argument mining, and human-centered NLP

Author Guidelines

Policies, formatting, responsible AI use, and student eligibility.

Submission Requirements

All submissions must be written in English, submitted as a single PDF, and formatted using the official ACL style templates. The final NAACL 2027 SRW submission template link is TBD.

  • Long papers: up to 8 pages of content, plus unlimited references and appendices.
  • Short papers: up to 4 pages of content, plus unlimited references and appendices.
  • Accepted papers may receive one additional content page for the camera-ready version, pending final policy.
  • Submissions that exceed page limits or violate formatting rules may be desk rejected.

Double-Blind Review

The review process will be double blind. Submissions must not identify authors, affiliations, funding, acknowledgments, or other identifying information. Self-citations should be written in the third person when needed to preserve anonymity.

Originality and Double Submission

Authors must accurately disclose related submissions, prior non-archival versions, and overlapping work in the submission form. Archival submissions must not violate NAACL, ACL, or other venue double-submission policies. Non-archival submissions may have more flexibility, subject to the final SRW policy.

Responsible Use of AI

Desk rejection policy: authors are responsible for all submitted content. Submissions may be desk rejected if AI tools are used in a way that fabricates results, obscures authorship, violates anonymity, introduces undisclosed generated material, creates false citations, or otherwise conflicts with ACL/NAACL publication ethics.

Authors may use writing, coding, or analysis tools only when the use is responsible, accurate, and disclosed where required by the final policy. AI assistance never transfers responsibility away from the authors.

Student Status Proof

The first author of each accepted paper must provide proof of student status at the camera-ready stage. Acceptable proof is expected to include one of the following:

  • A student ID card.
  • A current enrollment certificate or study confirmation.
  • A transcript or official institutional document.
  • For recent graduates or gap-year students: proof from the period when the work was completed.

Expected Camera-Ready Form Fields

Upload field: proof of student status or ID document, PDF/JPG/PNG.

Mentoring

Pre-submission feedback and career-oriented student support.

Pre-Submission Mentorship Program

The SRW will offer students the opportunity to receive feedback before submitting their work for review. The goal is to improve the writing, presentation, and clarity of the paper, not to decide whether the work should be accepted. Participation is optional but strongly encouraged, especially for students who are new to NLP research or new to ACL-style reviewing.

Pre-submission mentorship is not anonymous, but submitted drafts should still be anonymized. Mentors will not review the same paper during the final SRW review process.

Mentorship warning: the mentorship program exists to support students. It is not an appropriate venue for solicitation of any kind. The corresponding author for mentorship submissions must be a student.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions adapted from recent SRW guidance.

  • Do all authors have to be students?

    No. The first author must be a student, or must have completed the work while a student. Other authors may be non-students.

  • Can I submit a non-archival accepted paper to another venue later?

    Yes, provided the other venue allows it and all double-submission policies are followed.

  • Is ARR commitment available?

    No ARR commitment track is currently planned for NAACL 2027 SRW. The workshop is planned as direct submission only unless later announced otherwise.

  • If I submit to mentorship, do I also need to submit to the workshop?

    Yes. A mentorship draft is not a final workshop submission. Authors must submit the final paper through the SRW submission site.

  • What is the format for a thesis proposal?

    Thesis proposals should use the ACL long-paper format unless otherwise announced. The expected maximum is 8 content pages plus references and appendices.

  • Can a systematic literature survey be submitted?

    Yes, if it is student-led, relevant to computational linguistics/NLP, and the first author satisfies the student requirement.

  • What happens if the student author cannot attend in person?

    Presentation expectations, remote options, and travel support will be announced after coordination with NAACL 2027.

Travel and Grants

Financial support, visa guidance, and student travel information.

SRW Grant Application Guideline

We plan to invite eligible students to apply for SRW grants that may help cover registration, travel, or accommodation expenses. The funding situation and exact number of grants are TBD.

Priority Consideration

  • Students whose paper has been accepted and who are first authors.
  • Students presenting work at the SRW.
  • One funded author per accepted paper, subject to budget.
  • Students from developing countries or marginalized communities.
  • First-time NAACL/ACL-family conference attendees.
  • Students without other travel, registration, or diversity and inclusion funding.

What to Submit

Application materials are expected to include:

  • Basic personal and academic information.
  • A personal statement explaining need and motivation.
  • Optional supporting or justification materials.
  • Proof of accepted-paper status, if applicable.

Visa Process

NAACL 2027 will take place in the United States. Attendees who need a visa should begin the process as early as possible after acceptance or registration. Official invitation-letter procedures and conference visa guidance are TBD.
